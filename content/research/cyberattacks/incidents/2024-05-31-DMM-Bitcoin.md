## Summary

On May 31, 2024, Japanese cryptocurrency exchange DMM Bitcoin [detected an unauthorized outflow of Bitcoin from its wallet](https://web.archive.org/web/20241226193901/https://bitcoin.dmm.com/news/20240531_01) amounting to [4,502.9 BTC, approximately 48.2 billion yen ($305 million)](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2024/05/31/japanese-crypto-exchange-dmm-bitcoin-suffers-305m-hack) (the joint FBI/NPA advisory later valued the loss at [$308 million](https://www.npa.go.jp/bureau/cyber/pdf/20241224_caution.pdf) at the time of the attack). The exchange stated that the outflow was not caused by a breach of its core trading system, and immediately restricted new account screenings, crypto withdrawals, spot buy orders, and new leveraged positions. DMM Bitcoin [guaranteed all customer Bitcoin deposits in full](https://web.archive.org/web/20241226193901/https://bitcoin.dmm.com/news/20240531_01) and secured group-company support to procure replacement BTC, subsequently arranging [55 billion yen in loans (~$367 million)](https://therecord.media/fbi-largest-crypto-hack-2024-tradertraitor). Chainalysis ranked the incident as the [seventh-largest crypto hack ever recorded](https://x.com/chainalysis/status/1796571757341765916) at the time. On December 23-24, 2024, the FBI, DC3, and Japan's National Police Agency [jointly attributed the theft](https://www.npa.go.jp/bureau/cyber/pdf/20241224_caution.pdf) to the North Korean state-linked TraderTraitor threat group, considered part of the Lazarus Group. DMM Bitcoin [announced service termination](https://therecord.media/japanese-crypto-service-shuts-down) with accounts and assets [transferred to SBI VC Trade on March 8, 2025](https://www.sbivc.co.jp/newsview/273xz3k65), with the incident cited as contributing factor.

## Attackers

The theft was [jointly attributed](https://www.npa.go.jp/bureau/cyber/pdf/20241224_caution.pdf) on December 24, 2024 by Japan's National Police Agency (Kanto Regional Police Bureau National Cyber Department and the Tokyo Metropolitan Police Department) and the [FBI with DC3](https://www.fbi.gov/news/press-releases/fbi-dc3-and-npa-identification-of-north-korean-cyber-actors-tracked-as-tradertraitor-responsible-for-theft-of-308-million-from-bitcoindmmcom) to **TraderTraitor**, a North Korean cyber threat actor considered part of the Lazarus Group.

Per the joint investigation, in late March 2024 a North Korean actor impersonating a recruiter on LinkedIn [contacted an employee of Ginco](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20241225-dmm-bitcoin-north-korea/), a Japan-based cryptocurrency wallet software firm entrusted with managing DMM Bitcoin wallets, and sent a URL to a malicious Python script disguised as a recruitment test, which the employee copied onto a GitHub page. From May 2024, the actors [misused session cookie information](https://www.npa.go.jp/bureau/cyber/pdf/20241224_caution.pdf) to impersonate the employee and access Ginco's systems, then likely used this access to [manipulate a legitimate transaction request by a DMM employee](https://therecord.media/fbi-largest-crypto-hack-2024-tradertraitor), resulting in the loss of 4,502.9 BTC. The stolen funds ultimately moved to TraderTraitor-controlled wallets.

On-chain investigator [ZachXBT traced more than $35 million](https://coinedition.com/zachxbt-uncovers-new-evidence-in-305-million-dmm-bitcoin-hack-investigation/) of the stolen funds flowing to the Cambodian online marketplace Huione Guarantee, attributing the hack to Lazarus off-chain indicators. Per ZachXBT's analysis, the laundering **typically followed a pattern** of channeling the stolen BTC through a transaction mixer, then bridging from Bitcoin to Ethereum or Avalanche via THORChain, Threshold, or Avalanche Bridge before swapping into other assets; the public reporting describes this as the general laundering pattern observed, and no complete per-transaction attribution for every listed route has been published. Verifiable on-chain tracking facts include: [PeckShieldAlert](https://x.com/PeckShieldAlert/status/1826523531032887764) publicly tracked a DMM Bitcoin hacker-labeled address moving 500 BTC (~$30.4 million) to two new addresses on August 22, 2024, and [Chainalysis](https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/crypto-hacking-stolen-funds-2025/) published Reactor fund-flow graphs showing the attacker moving millions of dollars from DMM Bitcoin through intermediary addresses to a Bitcoin CoinJoin mixing service. In July 2024, [Tether blacklisted a Huione-linked wallet](https://coinedition.com/zachxbt-uncovers-new-evidence-in-305-million-dmm-bitcoin-hack-investigation/) holding 29.6 million USDT on Tron, into which approximately $14 million in hack proceeds had flowed within three days. Specific wallet addresses and transaction hashes were not disclosed in the public advisories or articles; the Chainalysis Reactor graphs are the primary published on-chain record.

## Losses

- [4,502.9 BTC](https://web.archive.org/web/20241226193901/https://bitcoin.dmm.com/news/20240531_01) stolen in a single unauthorized outflow, detected May 31, 2024, 13:26 JST
- Approximately [48.2 billion yen ($305 million)](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2024/05/31/japanese-crypto-exchange-dmm-bitcoin-suffers-305m-hack) per initial reports; [$308 million](https://www.npa.go.jp/bureau/cyber/pdf/20241224_caution.pdf) per the joint FBI/NPA advisory valuation at time of attack
- Ranked by Chainalysis as the [seventh-largest cryptocurrency hack in history](https://x.com/chainalysis/status/1796571757341765916) at the time, and the largest of 2024
- DMM Bitcoin covered losses with group-company procurement and [55 billion yen (~$367 million) in loans](https://therecord.media/fbi-largest-crypto-hack-2024-tradertraitor)
- Japan's Financial Services Agency investigation found ["serious problems" with DMM's system risk management](https://therecord.media/fbi-largest-crypto-hack-2024-tradertraitor) and its response to crypto asset leakage risk

## Timeline

- **Late March 2024:** North Korean actors impersonating a recruiter on LinkedIn contact a Ginco employee and send a URL to a malicious Python script disguised as a recruitment test, which the employee [copied to his own GitHub page and was subsequently compromised](https://www.npa.go.jp/bureau/cyber/pdf/20241224_caution.pdf), per the joint NPA/FBI investigation.
- **May 2024 onward:** Actors misuse session cookie information to impersonate the Ginco employee and access Ginco's communications system, [likely manipulating a legitimate DMM transaction request](https://therecord.media/fbi-largest-crypto-hack-2024-tradertraitor).
- **May 31, 2024, 13:26 JST:** DMM detects the unauthorized outflow of [4,502.9 BTC](https://web.archive.org/web/20241226193901/https://bitcoin.dmm.com/news/20240531_01) and announces restrictions: new account screening suspension, crypto withdrawal freeze, spot buys halted (sells only), no new leveraged positions. Customer BTC deposits guaranteed in full with group-company procurement.
- **May 31, 2024:** International press confirms the [$305 million loss](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2024/05/31/japanese-crypto-exchange-dmm-bitcoin-suffers-305m-hack); Chainalysis [ranks it 7th largest crypto hack ever](https://x.com/chainalysis/status/1796571757341765916).
- **June 2024:** DMM Group procures replacement BTC and secures [55 billion yen in loans (~$367 million)](https://therecord.media/fbi-largest-crypto-hack-2024-tradertraitor); Arkham Intelligence [offers a 1,000 ARKM token bounty](https://x.com/ArkhamIntel/status/1796563505782337949) for information.
- **July 2024:** [ZachXBT publishes tracing evidence](https://coinedition.com/zachxbt-uncovers-new-evidence-in-305-million-dmm-bitcoin-hack-investigation/) of $35M+ flowing to Huione Guarantee; Tether blacklists a Huione-linked wallet holding 29.6M USDT.
- **September 2024:** Japan's FSA completes its investigation, citing ["serious problems" in DMM's system risk management](https://therecord.media/fbi-largest-crypto-hack-2024-tradertraitor).
- **December 23-24, 2024:** The [FBI, DC3, and NPA jointly attribute](https://www.npa.go.jp/bureau/cyber/pdf/20241224_caution.pdf) the theft to North Korean TraderTraitor actors, detailing the LinkedIn social-engineering scheme against the Ginco employee and session-cookie abuse.
- **March 8, 2025:** DMM Bitcoin [terminates service](https://therecord.media/japanese-crypto-service-shuts-down); accounts and assets [transferred to SBI VC Trade](https://www.sbivc.co.jp/newsview/273xz3k65).

## Security Failure Causes

**Supply-chain social engineering of a wallet-management vendor:** The initial access was obtained not from DMM directly but from [Ginco](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20241225-dmm-bitcoin-north-korea/), the wallet-software firm entrusted with DMM wallet management, via a fake-recruiter LinkedIn approach and a malicious Python "recruitment test" script — the [documented TraderTraitor tradecraft](https://therecord.media/fbi-largest-crypto-hack-2024-tradertraitor) of impersonating developers/recruiters to target cryptocurrency-firm employees.

**Session hijacking instead of key theft:** From May 2024, the actors abused [session cookie information](https://www.npa.go.jp/bureau/cyber/pdf/20241224_caution.pdf) to impersonate the compromised employee, then likely manipulated a legitimate transaction request by a DMM employee. The joint advisories do not describe a private-key compromise; transaction-approval workflow integrity was the failure point.

**Transaction-approval manipulation:** The loss of 4,502.9 BTC resulted from a [legitimate transaction request being manipulated](https://therecord.media/fbi-largest-crypto-hack-2024-tradertraitor) through compromised vendor access. The public reporting does not detail the wallet-approval controls in place, so the specific control failure (e.g., insufficient independent verification of large withdrawal requests) is an inference from the manipulation timeline rather than an established finding.

**Laundering via mixers and Huione Guarantee:** Post-theft tracing by [Chainalysis](https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/crypto-hacking-stolen-funds-2025/) showed the stolen BTC moved through anonymizing mixers and was funneled to the Cambodian marketplace Huione Guarantee; intervention such as the Tether blacklist of a recipient wallet occurred only after funds had dispersed. ZachXBT's published description of the mixer-and-bridge sequence is the attributed general laundering pattern; per-transaction evidence for each route was not publicly disclosed, and on-chain tracking milestones (the [PeckShieldAlert 500 BTC move](https://x.com/PeckShieldAlert/status/1826523531032887764), the Chainalysis Reactor graphs) are the verifiable public record.
