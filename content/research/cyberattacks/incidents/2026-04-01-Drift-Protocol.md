## Summary

- On April 1, 2026 at approximately [16:05 UTC](https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/lessons-from-the-drift-hack/), an attacker took over the admin authority of [Drift Protocol](https://www.drift.trade/), the largest decentralized perpetual futures exchange on Solana, and drained a confirmed [$285.26 million across 18 vault assets in 128 seconds](https://www.quillaudits.com/blog/hack-analysis/drift-protocol-multisig-exploit) — over 50% of protocol TVL.
- The attack chain combined [a six-month DPRK-linked social engineering operation against Drift contributors](https://x.com/DriftProtocol/status/2040611161121370409), abuse of [Solana durable nonce pre-signed transactions](https://cointelegraph.com/news/drift-280-million-hack-questions-circle-response) to obtain admin transfer signatures from Security Council members, a [recent migration to a 2-of-5 multisig with zero timelock](https://gist.github.com/andrewhong5297/512c55864505f0c6caaece9e9b07b7c3), and [oracle manipulation via a fake attacker-controlled collateral token (CarbonVote, "CVT")](https://rekt.news/drift-protocol-rekt/).
- Drift's Security Council, working with SEAL 911, assessed with medium-high confidence that the operation was carried out by [UNC4736, a DPRK state-affiliated group also tracked as AppleJeus or Citrine Sleet](https://x.com/DriftProtocol/status/2040611161121370409), linked on-chain to the actors behind the October 2024 Radiant Capital hack. TRM Labs ([April 2](https://www.trmlabs.com/resources/blog/north-korean-hackers-attack-drift-protocol-in-285-million-heist)) and Elliptic ([via CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2026/04/02/north-koreans-hackers-likely-behind-the-usd286-million-drift-protocol-exploit-elliptic)) independently assessed the attackers as likely DPRK-linked; per [Chainalysis](https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/lessons-from-the-drift-hack/), preliminary indicators were consistent with prior DPRK operations.

## Attackers

- On April 4, 2026, Drift published an [Incident Background Update](https://x.com/DriftProtocol/status/2040611161121370409) describing a structured intelligence operation beginning around October 2025: actors posing as a quantitative trading firm approached Drift contributors in person at a major crypto event, built professional relationships over roughly six months, set up a Telegram group, and deposited over $1 million of their own capital into an onboarding "Ecosystem Vault" to establish legitimacy.
- As integration discussions deepened, the group shared repositories and TestFlight applications described as their frontend/wallet product. One contributor may have cloned a shared code repository; another may have installed a TestFlight application. Drift noted it has not confirmed which vector succeeded; for the repository vector it pointed to publicly flagged vulnerabilities in VSCode/Cursor that allow arbitrary code execution when opening untrusted files or folders.
- Attribution: Drift/SEAL 911 assessed [UNC4736 (also tracked as AppleJeus / Citrine Sleet)](https://x.com/DriftProtocol/status/2040611161121370409) with medium-high confidence, based on fund-flow overlap with the [October 2024 Radiant Capital (~$53M) hack](https://www.halborn.com/blog/post/explained-the-radiant-capital-hack-october-2024). TRM Labs and [Elliptic (per CoinDesk)](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2026/04/02/north-koreans-hackers-likely-behind-the-usd286-million-drift-protocol-exploit-elliptic) independently assessed likely DPRK attribution. Per Drift, the individuals appearing in person were not North Korean nationals; DPRK operations are known to use third-party intermediaries with constructed identities.

## Timeline

- **~October 2025:** A group posing as a quantitative trading firm [approached Drift contributors at a crypto event](https://x.com/DriftProtocol/status/2040611161121370409) and began months of relationship-building, including a Telegram group.
- **December 2025 – January 2026:** The group [onboarded an "Ecosystem Vault" on Drift and deposited over $1 million of its own capital](https://www.theblock.co/post/396361/drift-links-280-million-exploit-to-six-month-social-engineering-op-run-by-suspected-north-korean-actors) to build legitimacy.
- **March 12, 2026:** The attacker created the CarbonVote (CVT) token ([mint address](https://solscan.io/token/G84LEhbNMR1yYbHgHbnNYNSK8mpTKcazh5jcW5yMPQKo)), minting hundreds of millions of units for ~$1.19 and seeding a Raydium pool with ~$500, then wash-traded it to build a near-$1 price history ([Chainalysis](https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/lessons-from-the-drift-hack/)).
- **March 23:** Four [durable nonce accounts were created](https://x.com/DriftProtocol/status/2039564444510846997): two attacker-controlled, two linked to legitimate members of Drift's Security Council multisig.
- **Late March:** Drift executed a planned [Security Council migration replacing four of five signers and lowering the threshold from 3-of-5 to 2-of-5](https://gist.github.com/andrewhong5297/512c55864505f0c6caaece9e9b07b7c3); within days the attacker obtained a pre-signed nonce from one of the replacement signers.
- **April 1, 2026, ~16:05 UTC:** The pre-signed transactions fired — two transactions four slots apart transferred Drift's State account admin ([tx 1](https://solscan.io/tx/2HvMSgDEfKhNryYZKhjowrBY55rUx5MWtcWkG9hqxZCFBaTiahPwfynP1dxBSRk9s5UTVc8LFeS4Btvkm9pc2C4H), [tx 2](https://solscan.io/tx/4BKBmAJn6TdsENij7CsVbyMVLJU1tX27nfrMM1zgKv1bs2KJy6Am2NqdA3nJm4g9C6eC64UAf5sNs974ygB9RsN1)); the attacker created a maximally-permissive CVT collateral market, raised withdrawal guards to 500 trillion across five markets ([tx](https://solscan.io/tx/4a5962Rdqd9pkXtk9DMQ9ZYhdGb2k9gPw71GvukJgELhxbCY5gm1c1hhKdwuGefyqJ3XMvihUTDNDn3qbXnst82X)), and drained 18 assets in 128 seconds ([QuillAudits](https://www.quillaudits.com/blog/hack-analysis/drift-protocol-multisig-exploit)). [Drift suspended deposits and withdrawals](https://x.com/DriftProtocol/status/2039417136729227425).
- **April 2, 2026:** [TRM Labs](https://www.trmlabs.com/resources/blog/north-korean-hackers-attack-drift-protocol-in-285-million-heist) and [Elliptic (per CoinDesk)](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2026/04/02/north-koreans-hackers-likely-behind-the-usd286-million-drift-protocol-exploit-elliptic) publicly attributed the theft to likely DPRK actors.
- **April 4, 2026:** [Drift published its Incident Background Update](https://x.com/DriftProtocol/status/2040611161121370409) describing the six-month operation and the UNC4736 assessment (medium-high confidence).
- **April 9, 2026:** [Chainalysis published its analysis](https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/lessons-from-the-drift-hack/); Drift posted an [interim update](https://x.com/DriftProtocol/status/2042093972500177305) acknowledging impact on users and builders.
- **April 16, 2026:** Drift published an [incident recovery update](https://www.drift.trade/updates/incident-recovery-update-april-16-2026-now) with a proposed recovery framework, partner funding, and a total loss table of $295,706,374.93 including 125,000 SOL ($10.43M).

## Security Failure Causes

- **Six-month social engineering campaign (initial access):** Per [Drift's own postmortem](https://x.com/DriftProtocol/status/2040611161121370409), operators built in-person working relationships with contributors over roughly six months, then shared repositories/TestFlight applications that may have carried a code-execution vector. Drift pointed to known VSCode/Cursor file-open vulnerabilities flagged since late 2025 but did not confirm which vector succeeded; both remained under forensic investigation.
- **Durable nonce pre-signature misrepresentation:** Solana's durable nonce feature let the attackers hold valid pre-signed transactions from Security Council members indefinitely. Signers [approved transactions they did not realize contained hidden admin authorizations](https://x.com/DriftProtocol/status/2039564448956858642) — what Drift characterized as targeted social engineering or transaction misrepresentation ([CoinTelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/drift-280-million-hack-questions-circle-response)).
- **Zero-timelock 2-of-5 Security Council migration:** Days before execution, Drift migrated its multisig from 3-of-5 to [2-of-5 with no timelock](https://gist.github.com/andrewhong5297/512c55864505f0c6caaece9e9b07b7c3), removing any review window. TRM flagged removal of this delay as a central safety failure; peer protocols used 12-hour+ timelocks ([comparison by Fabiano](https://x.com/fabianosolana/status/2039657017825017970)).
- **Oracle manipulation via attacker-controlled collateral:** The fake CVT token, wash-traded to build a credible price history and priced by an oracle the attacker themselves deployed ([Chainalysis](https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/lessons-from-the-drift-hack/)), was accepted as collateral worth hundreds of millions once the attacker held admin rights. TRM cited this as evidence for minimum-liquidity thresholds, time-weighted pricing, and circuit breakers.
- **Concentrated admin authority without layered controls:** The compromised signer authority controlled market creation, oracle assignment, and withdrawal limits directly; Chaos Labs' Omer Goldberg noted [the full sequence took less than 15 seconds](https://x.com/omeragoldberg/status/2039455335858012435). Neodyme's 2024 security review of Drift examined this authority structure but treated it as an acceptable trust assumption ([review commentary](https://x.com/officer_secret/status/2039409295075226021)) — no code audit catches a human compromise.
