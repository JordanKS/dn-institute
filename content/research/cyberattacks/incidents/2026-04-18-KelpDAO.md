## Summary

On April 18, 2026 at 17:35 UTC (Ethereum block 24,908,285), an attacker [forged a LayerZero cross-chain message](https://www.theblock.co/post/397988/kelp-daos-rseth-bridge-apparently-exploited-for-roughly-292-million-in-layerzero-based-attack) to release 116,500 rsETH — about 15% of circulating supply, worth roughly [$290-292 million](https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/kelpdao-bridge-exploit-april-2026/) — from KelpDAO's Ethereum bridge escrow in a single transaction. The stolen tokens were immediately pledged as collateral on Aave, Compound, and Euler; the attacker borrowed an estimated $236 million in WETH and wstETH and laundered the proceeds through Tornado Cash. The drain triggered a run on Aave, with stablecoin lenders alone withdrawing about [$6.3 billion](https://dailyhodl.com/2026/04/19/6280000000-abruptly-exits-ethereum-based-lending-platform-as-cross-chain-hack-triggers-mass-withdrawals/), and DeFi total value locked fell roughly [$13-15 billion](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/04/20/defi-tvl-drops-more-than-usd13-billion-in-two-days-following-kelp-dao-hack/) in the following two days. It was the largest DeFi exploit of 2026 and the largest crypto hack since the February 2025 Bybit incident. The root cause was not a smart-contract bug but a compromise of off-chain LayerZero verification infrastructure.

## Attackers

LayerZero preliminarily attributed the operation to the DPRK-linked [Lazarus Group, specifically its TraderTraitor subgroup](https://www.coindesk.com/tech/2026/04/20/layerzero-blames-kelp-s-setup-for-usd290-million-exploit-attributes-it-to-north-korea-s-lazarus). It was the second nine-figure DeFi theft tied to the same state group in eighteen days, after Drift Protocol (April 1, ~$285 million); together they extracted more than $575 million in under three weeks. The drain required no human compromise: a node list, two poisoned RPC binaries, a DDoS, and one forged packet, per [Galaxy Research's analysis](https://www.galaxy.com/insights/research/kelpdao-layerzero-exploit-defi).

## Losses

- [116,500 rsETH (~$290-292 million)](https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/kelpdao-bridge-exploit-april-2026/), about 15% of supply, drained in a single forged release.
- Of that, 89,567 rsETH (~$221 million) was deposited on Aave as collateral, funding loans of [82,650 WETH (~$190.9 million) and 821 wstETH (~$2.3 million)](https://governance.aave.com/t/rseth-2026/24580).
- The cashout used collateral borrowing rather than a DEX sell-off, so realized value was ~$200-236 million; roughly $175 million reached Bitcoin rails within days per [Chainalysis](https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/kelpdao-bridge-exploit-april-2026/).
- Aave's estimated bad debt was [~$123.7 million under uniform loss socialization](https://governance.aave.com/t/rseth-2026/24580), up to ~$230 million if losses fall on bridged L2-issued rsETH; $177 million is the most cited median per the [Aave incident report](https://governance.aave.com/t/rseth-2026/24580).
- The bridge invariant broke: the escrow adapter fell from 116,723 to 223 rsETH, leaving 112,204 unbacked against 152,577 remote-chain claims — an implied ~15% depeg for the wrapper, per [Galaxy Research](https://www.galaxy.com/insights/research/kelpdao-layerzero-exploit-defi).

## Timeline

- **17:35 UTC, April 18:** Forged packet claiming Unichain origin was accepted by a single 1-of-1 DVN without any matching source burn, releasing [116,500 rsETH](https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/kelpdao-bridge-exploit-april-2026/).
- **Within minutes:** The tokens fanned across branch addresses and into Aave plus other lending venues; early estimates topped $100 million ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/397988/kelp-daos-rseth-bridge-apparently-exploited-for-roughly-292-million-in-layerzero-based-attack)).
- **~46 minutes in:** A second forged packet (~40,000 rsETH, ~$95 million) reverted because the protocol had already paused contracts and frozen addresses ([Chainalysis](https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/kelpdao-bridge-exploit-april-2026/)).
- **Later April 18:** Aave's Protocol Guardian froze rsETH and wrapped-rsETH reserves to zero LTV across all deployments, then froze WETH borrows within a day ([Aave governance](https://governance.aave.com/t/rseth-2026/24580)).
- **April 20:** The Arbitrum Security Council [froze 30,766 ETH](https://x.com/arbitrum/status/2046435443680346189) of the exploiter's coins; LayerZero published a [post-mortem](https://x.com/LayerZero_Core/status/2046081551574983137).
- **By weekend close:** Total DeFi TVL had dropped [$13-15 billion](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/04/20/defi-tvl-drops-more-than-usd13-billion-in-two-days-following-kelp-dao-hack/); Aave reopened core ETH loans and loss allocation shifted to governance.

## Security Failure Causes

**Single-verifier configuration (a single point of failure):** Kelp deployed rsETH with one required verifier and zero optional ones, letting one forged attestation authorize every release with no independent check — [LayerZero placed blame on this setup](https://www.coindesk.com/tech/2026/04/20/layerzero-blames-kelp-s-setup-for-usd290-million-exploit-attributes-it-to-north-korea-s-lazarus).

**The validation layer sat off-chain, outside any audit scope:** Attackers modified the RPC backend the DVN used, fed it fabricated burn events, and DDoS'd the real endpoints so the verifier only saw attacker-controlled data, per [Galaxy Research](https://www.galaxy.com/insights/research/kelpdao-layerzero-exploit-defi). Standard smart-contract audits missed all of it.

**The cross-chain invariant was never monitored:** A release on Ethereum with no corresponding burn on Unichain is invisible to per-transaction checks; it needs cross-chain reconciliation comparing mints against burns, which was absent ([Chainalysis](https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/kelpdao-bridge-exploit-april-2026/)).

**Composability amplified the failure:** rsETH was collateral across ~20 chains and major lending venues, so a single packet created bad debt at protocols that were never breached and drove a multi-billion-dollar run at Aave — the case study of DeFi's dependence on third-party collateral ([Bank Policy Institute](https://bpi.com/crypto-hacks-and-defi-runs/)).
